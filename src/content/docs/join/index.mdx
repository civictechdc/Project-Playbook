The best way to get involved with Civic Tech DC is to just show up!

Civic Tech DC is a project-driven community. At any given time, we have 6–7 active projects each with a team of volunteers and working on a real civic problem. We meet twice a month for **Project Night**, where people come together to work on these projects.

**Everyone is welcome.** You don't need to be a developer, have a technical background, or know exactly what you want to work on. Many people come to their first Project Night simply because they're curious about civic technology and want to meet people doing the work.

## What is a Project?

A **project** is a service, tool, or process that uses technology to address a local community need. All of our projects are rooted in a **real problem experienced by a community** and are developed in partnership with a **validated partner** who helps us understand the problem and determine whether what we're building is actually useful.

A project can use and apply technology in many different ways, ranging from a website, data visualization map, research tools, etc. Each project focuses on understanding:

- **Problem:** What community need or problem are we trying to address?
- **People:** Who is affected by the problem and who are our community partners?
- **Technology:** What tools are we using?
- **Process:** How do we self-organize and make decisions?
- **Impact:** How do we know whether we're making a difference?

## Find a Project

We’re always incubating new projects and iterating on existing ones. You can explore the full list of [active projects here](https://www.civictechdc.org/projects).

Each project operates a little differently. Some have structured onboarding <img
class="civic-tech-float"
src="/images/projectplaybook2.png"

/>
and clearly defined tasks. Others are starting and, as such, are more exploratory in relying on contributors to help figure out what needs to happen next. Don't be afraid to ask questions to the team; new people join all the time, and they want to help you feel comfortable! 

You also are not required to stay with the project you first join; you're free to move between projects!

## A. Choosing a Project

On your first night, feel free to explore multiple projects. Make sure to get a feel for each team by asking questions and learning about current needs. As you explore projects, here are a few things to think about:
- **The Problem:** Does the community need or challenge the project is addressing interest you? You don't need to understand the technology yet; start first with the problem the team is trying to solve.
- **The Peoplem:** Do the team's interests, working style, and approach appeal to you? 
- **Work Involved:** What does the project need help with right now? Projects need more than software developers. Depending on the team's needs, you might contribute through design, research, writing, outreach, data analysis, or project coordination.
- **Stage of the Project:** Is the team exploring an early idea, building something new, or maintaining an existing tool? Different stages offer different opportunities to contribute.
- **Your Time:** How much time can you realistically commit, and what would you like to learn or practice? Look for a role that fits your current bandwidth while giving you room to grow.

And of course, you don't have to commit to one project. Talk to the project lead and ask what a first contribution might look like. You're welcome to move between different projects as your interests evolve.

## B. Officially Joining A Project

If you find a project you're interested in, talk to the project lead or another member of the team to share your interest. 

They will usually point you to Slack because that is where the asynchronous work between project nights happens. From there, your first few steps will usually look something like:

### Your Bandwidth 

Civic Tech DC runs entirely on volunteer energy, and we want to make sure that if you need to step away, you feel okay to do so. 
Civic Tech DC is structured so that beginner contributions are small and flexible to help ease you into a project. If life gets busy 
and you need to step back, just let someone know! We completely understand.

### Project Logstics 

- **Bi-weekly Project Nights:**
We meet on the 2nd and 4th Wednesdays of every month. To register and find the next Project Night, check out our [calendar](https://luma.com/civic-tech-dc).

- **Join Slack:**
Slack is the best way to stay connected between Project Nights with your project. The community also shares updates, coordinates work, and posts announcements. It is extremely recommended that you join. If you’re new to Slack, [here’s the official guide](https://slack.com/help/articles/360059928654-How-to-use-Slack%E2%80%94your-quick-start-guide).

- **Join GitHub:**
All projects have a GitHub repository under the main [Civic Tech DC](https://github.com/civictechdc) account. Each project uses GitHub to various amounts of use. It is recommended that you have an awareness of GitHub and consider creating an account with [instructions here](https://github.com/signup). If you have a GitHub account, ask the project leads to add your username.

- **Project Documentation:**
Projects may maintain documentation in different places, including GitHub, shared documents, project websites, or other tools. Your project lead can point you toward the resources you need.

- **Code of Conduct:**
Civic Tech DC is committed to creating a welcoming and inclusive community. Our [code of conduct](https://www.civictechdc.org/code-of-conduct) describes the behavior we expect from community members and how concerns can be reported.

If you have a concern, please come talk to our [team](https://www.civictechdc.org/about). Or if you want to be anonymous, you can fill out [this form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeyyeiA7P6FgegxwRFaHmBHmgZyZktja79cY9Dvo7PGyJOpEA/viewform), and we will take the next best steps.

### More Than A Project 

Civic Tech DC is an opportunity to work on real problems and connect with people who care about making technology work better for their communities. What you take away will depend on how you get involved, but there are plenty of ways to grow along the way.

- Contribute to open-source projects that address real community needs, with publicly documented work you can share in a portfolio or discuss in an interview.
- Meet people across DC's civic tech community and learn from the diverse perspectives they bring.
- Try a new technical skill, practice design research, facilitate a team discussion, or help shape a project's direction.
- See how civic technology works in practice: Learn how teams identify community needs or work with partners.
