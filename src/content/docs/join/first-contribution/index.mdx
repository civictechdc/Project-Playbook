title: Your First Contribution
description: Find a first task, work with a project team, and stay involved at your own pace.
---

You don't need to know exactly what you want to do before coming to Project Night. The first step is finding a project that interests you and learning what the team needs help with.

Every project works a little differently. Some have tasks ready for new contributors, while others may still be figuring out their next steps. Your first contribution does not have to be a big one.

## Start With the Project

At Project Night, listen to the project pitches and visit a team that interests you. Ask the project lead or another contributor what they're working on and what help they need right now.

It can help to share a little about yourself:

- **Your interests:** What problem or type of work brought you here?
- **Your skills:** What experience do you have, whether technical or not?
- **What you want to learn:** Is there something new you'd like to try?
- **Your time:** Can you help during Project Night, between meetings, or both?

If you're still exploring different ways to help, see [Choose Your Contribution Path](/resources/contributionpaths/). The paths are examples, not formal roles that every team needs.

## Find a First Task

A good first task is small enough to understand and complete without having to learn everything about a project.

Depending on what the team needs, you might:

- Test a feature and share what was confusing.
- Review a document or improve instructions for new contributors.
- Help research a public problem or check a dataset.
- Take notes from a discussion and organize next steps.
- Fix a small issue or learn how to run the project locally.
- Help prepare questions for a community partner or user interview.

Not every project will have all of these opportunities. Ask what would be useful **for that team right now**, rather than assuming a particular role is available.

Before starting, make sure you understand what the task is, who to ask for help, and how the team wants you to share your work. It's okay to ask questions or say that a task is bigger than you expected.

## Stay Connected Between Project Nights

Most teams use Slack to share updates and coordinate work between meetings. Some also use GitHub, shared documents, or other tools. Your project lead can point you toward the right place.

If you take on a task, a short update can go a long way. Let the team know what you've tried, where you're stuck, or whether you need more time. You don't have to wait until something is finished to communicate.

If you can't make the next Project Night, that's okay. Just let someone know if other people are waiting on your work.

## Find a Rhythm That Works for You

Civic Tech DC runs on volunteer time. As a general guideline, volunteers should not be expected to contribute more than 10 hours per week. Some people only participate during Project Nights, while others take on tasks between meetings.

You can start small, try a different type of contribution, or explore another project. You are not expected to make a long-term commitment on your first night.

**The goal of your first contribution isn't to prove how much you can do.** It's to get to know the team, contribute something useful, and figure out whether the project is a good fit for you.
