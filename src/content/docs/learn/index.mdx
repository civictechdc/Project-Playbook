In October 2012, DC folks showed up to the first meeting of what would become Code for DC. People came ready to build solutions, but cofounder Justin Grimes started with a question instead: [“Do you engage civically?”](https://technical.ly/civic-news/help-code-dc-14-civic-hacking-projects/)

Most of the room couldn’t say which ward they lived in, let alone their [Advisory Neighborhood Commission (ANC)](https://oanc.dc.gov/), D.C.’s neighborhood bodies of local government. With that, Grimes led a team to build ANC Finder, a simple tool that tells you what ANC you are part of and how to contact your representative. It was later recommended on the DC Board of Ethics and Government Accountability website.

Notice what happened there. The folks started with a question about civic life, found a challenge their neighbors were experiencing, and then use technology to fix it.

That’s where civic tech begins for us: start with what the people care about. 

## Civic Tech 101

Civic tech is a broad field, and even if you couldn’t define it, you’ve probably run into it somewhere. If you’ve looked up your trash pickup day online, tracked a bill through Congress, or used a map to find your local grocery store, you’ve touched it.

The term is made up of two parts, civics (the ways people come together to shape their communities) and technology, and neither works without the other. The movement is expansive, with many fields and approaches spinning off from it, including govtech, digital democracy, and campaign tech. Different organizations and individuals have defined it in ways that overlap but emphasize different parts of the field and the problems they want to solve. A few prominent voices describe it as:

- <i>“Use of digital tools and platforms that help people engage with governments and public institutions in more open, inclusive, and meaningful ways.” </i> [United Nations Development Programme](https://www.undp.org/asia-pacific/civic-tech)
- <i>“The use of newly available possibilities for the common good.” </i> [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/about)

Here at Civic Tech DC, we define civic tech as:

> **People using technology to strengthen civic life.**


For us, this definition starts with people and civic problems. We focus first on the processes and systems that shape civic life through human-centered design and then consider where technology can help. The volunteer projects we build are designed to be:

Our range of work is wide. ANC Finder was one of our earliest tools, but we’ve also built [dozens of tools](https://www.civictechdc.org/archive-dig/) ranging from 
[OurDCSchools.org](https://e-pluribusunum.org/2014/04/23/code-for-dc-launches-ourdcschools-org-an-open-government-platform-for-proposed-school-policy/) 
with support from the Deputy Mayor for Education (DME), and DC Crash Bots, which was featured and nominated for a 
[2021 Technical.ly Award](https://technical.ly/dc/2021/05/07/this-code-for-dc-project-is-updating-the-citys-car-crash-data-for-safer-streets/). Some projects are websites and web apps, some are data analysis, and some are workshops or designed materials.

At Civic Tech DC, a project does not need to be a sophisticated app or platform. What we measure success in is whether people are using available tools and approaches to solve civic problems.

## So, How We Engage Civically?

The name of the organization has changed from Code for DC to Civic Tech DC since that first meeting in 2012, but the principles that drive our work has not. 
Everyone is welcome to our community, and you don’t need to write code to start, just a willingness to work with others on an issue you care about.

## Explore Civic Tech

Curious to go deeper into the field of civic tech? Explore these books and resources:

- **[Open Government Data](https://opengovdata.io/)** by Josh Tauberer: Learn how open government data can improve transparency, accountability, and public participation.
- **[Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/)** by Matt Stempeck and contributors: Explore a global directory of civic tech projects, organizations, tools, and communities.
- **[Recoding America](https://www.recodingamerica.us/)** by Jennifer Pahlka: Learn why government services can fail and how better technology and public-sector practices can improve them.
- **[Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/resources/)**: Find practical guidance on digital government, accessibility, open data, and public service delivery.
- **[Human-Centered Design Guides](https://digital.gov/guides/hcd)**: Learn how to research community needs and design services around people's experiences.
- **[Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/)**: Explore how technology and human-centered design can improve government services and public benefits.
- **[U.S. Web Design System](https://designsystem.digital.gov/)**: Learn how to build accessible, consistent, and mobile-friendly government websites.
- **[18F Guides](https://guides.18f.gov/)**: Explore guidance on agile development, user research, and building better government digital services.
- **[Public Digital](https://public.digital/)**: Explore how digital transformation, technology, and organizational change can improve public services.
