Civic Tech DC projects should be designed around realistic volunteer availability.
As a general guideline, volunteers should not be expected to contribute more than 10 hours per week.
Project leads should avoid creating processes or deadlines that require volunteers to maintain a full-time pace.


<p class="lead">
You don't need to be a software developer to contribute to Civic Tech DC.
</p>

<p>
Our projects need people with different skills, experiences, and interests. You might write code, research a problem, talk with community members, organize a project, design an interface, analyze data, write documentation, or help tell the story of what a project is accomplishing.
</p>

<p>
You also don't need to commit to a specific role forever. Projects change, and your role can change with them.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Choose the path that sounds most like you.</strong>
</p>

---

## I Like Building Things

### Developer / Technologist

You like turning ideas into working tools, websites, applications, or systems.

**You might help with:**

- Frontend or backend development
- Data pipelines and analysis
- APIs and integrations
- Databases
- Testing and debugging
- Deployment and infrastructure
- Improving an existing project

**Good fit if you:** enjoy programming, technical problem-solving, or learning new technologies.

You don't need to know the project's technology already. Many projects have smaller issues that are a good way to learn the codebase.

---

## I Like Design

### Designer / UX

You help make projects easier and more accessible for the people who use them.

**You might help with:**

- User research
- User flows and wireframes
- Visual design
- Prototyping
- Accessibility
- Design systems
- Website and project materials

**Good fit if you:** enjoy understanding how people interact with technology and turning complex ideas into clear experiences.

---

## I Like Researching Problems

### Researcher / Analyst

You help projects understand the problem they are trying to solve.

**You might help with:**

- Background research
- Policy research
- User research
- Data analysis
- Literature reviews
- Mapping stakeholders
- Evaluating existing tools or approaches
- Documenting findings

**Good fit if you:** like asking questions, investigating problems, working with information, or understanding how systems work.

---

## I Like Working With People

### Community & Partnership Engagement

You help projects stay connected to the people and organizations they are trying to serve.

**You might help with:**

- Finding and talking with community partners
- Conducting user interviews
- Organizing community conversations
- Building relationships with nonprofits or public agencies
- Gathering feedback
- Supporting project outreach
- Connecting projects with other organizations

**Good fit if you:** enjoy talking with people, building relationships, and helping make sure projects are grounded in real community needs.

---

## I Like Keeping Things Organized

### Project Management

You help keep a project moving.

**You might help with:**

- Defining project goals
- Breaking work into manageable tasks
- Organizing meetings
- Planning milestones
- Tracking progress
- Coordinating contributors
- Following up on action items
- Helping the team stay focused on priorities

**Good fit if you:** like organizing people and information, keeping track of details, and helping teams turn ideas into action.

You do not need to be a professional project manager to help with project coordination.

---

## I Like Writing

### Documentation & Communications

You help make projects understandable and accessible to contributors, partners, and the broader community.

**You might help with:**

- README files
- Contributor guides
- Project documentation
- Tutorials
- Blog posts
- Project updates
- Presentations
- Case studies
- Grant or other public-facing materials

**Good fit if you:** enjoy explaining things, writing clearly, or turning complicated information into something other people can understand.

---

## I Like Working With Data

### Data & Analysis

You help projects collect, organize, understand, and communicate information.

**You might help with:**

- Data cleaning
- Data analysis
- Data visualization
- Mapping
- Data collection
- Research datasets
- Building dashboards
- Documenting data sources and methods

**Good fit if you:** enjoy working with data, spreadsheets, statistics, maps, or finding patterns in information.

---

## I Like Outreach & Storytelling

### Communications & Outreach

You help people discover Civic Tech DC projects and understand why they matter.

**You might help with:**

- Social media
- Newsletters
- Project announcements
- Presentations
- Events
- Community outreach
- Finding potential contributors
- Sharing project stories

**Good fit if you:** enjoy communicating ideas, meeting people, creating content, or helping projects reach the right audiences.

---

## I Have a Specific Skill

### Bring Your Own Role

Civic tech projects often need skills that don't fit neatly into a job title.

**You might contribute through:**

- Translation
- Accessibility
- Legal research
- Policy expertise
- Grant writing
- Facilitation
- Public speaking
- Event planning
- Quality assurance
- Data governance
- Security
- Subject-matter expertise
- Something else entirely

If you have a skill that you think could help a project, tell us what you can do. We can work with project leads to figure out where it could be useful.

---

## I Want to Do a Little of Everything

### Multiple Roles

You don't have to choose just one role.

Many Civic Tech DC contributors work across multiple areas depending on what a project needs. For example, you might:

- Code and help with project management
- Conduct research and design
- Analyze data and create visualizations
- Work with a community partner and write documentation
- Organize contributors and help with outreach

Your role can also change as the project develops.

---

## I'm New to Civic Tech

### First-Time Contributor

You do not need previous civic tech experience to participate.

A good first contribution might be:

- Attending a Project Night
- Reading a project's README
- Asking questions
- Helping with documentation
- Testing a project
- Working on a `good-first-issue`
- Joining a research or design task
- Helping with community outreach

Start by telling the project lead what you're interested in and how much time you have available. They can help you find an appropriate first contribution.

<p>
<strong>Everything counts.</strong> Projects need more than code to succeed.
</p>

---

## I'm Not Sure Yet

### That's okay.

You can come to a Project Night, look through current projects, and talk with project leads and other contributors.

You can also tell us:

- What you're interested in learning
- What skills you already have
- How much time you have available
- Whether you prefer working independently or with a team
- Whether you want to contribute occasionally or take on an ongoing role

You don't need to know exactly where you fit before you show up.

<p>
<strong>Start by exploring a project that interests you.</strong>
</p>
