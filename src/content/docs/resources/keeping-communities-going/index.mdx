While much of this playbook shares lessons from running Civic Tech DC and its projects, this section focuses on supporting other volunteer-driven communities. The DMV is home to dozens of fantastic meetups, neighborhood initiatives, and professional networks. Despite their different missions, many face similar challenges around growth, sustainability, and keeping people engaged.

Supporting other communities has long been part of Civic Tech DC’s history. In the past, we served as a fiscal sponsor for local groups, and more recently, we participated in the inaugural DMV Super Meetup to connect with other tech communities across the region.

We believe strong communities do more than sustain themselves; they uplift others. In that spirit, this in-progress guide shares tips and tricks for other communities.

## Creating a Routine

Civic Tech DC meets on the second and fourth Wednesdays of each month, giving people a predictable schedule to connect and work on projects. Consider establishing a regular meeting schedule for your community, whether that's every two weeks or once a month. You don't always need a planned activity. Low-key gatherings, like a happy hour at a local bar, can be just as valuable. Many places in DC can accommodate small groups, but if you're expecting a larger crowd, it's worth calling ahead.

## Host Different Types of Events

Civic Tech DC offers more than just regular project nights. Members can also participate in special events like hackathons, workshops, and activities organized by individual projects. Mixing up your events keeps things interesting and gives people different ways to get involved.

One surprisingly easy event to organize is a demo night, where community members take the lead by sharing what they've been working on. People generally enjoy showing off their work, and it takes some of the pressure off organizers to plan the content. You'll still need to recruit presenters and coordinate the schedule, but you don't have to do everything yourself!

## Be Honest About What You've Learned

It can feel scary to talk openly about what went wrong and what you've learned, but people appreciate hearing about the challenges as much as the successes. That's one reason we created this playbook: to share not only what has worked for Civic Tech DC, but also what we've struggled with along the way. Being open about these experiences helps bridge the gap between organizers and community members and encourages others to feel inspired to take on leadership roles.

## Learning From Your Members

Ask your members for feedback, but don't stop at just collecting it! Make sure to close the feedback loop by sharing how their suggestions were considered and what changes, if any, were made. You can gather feedback through post-event surveys, anonymous forms, or casual conversations, then share updates through your community's communication channels.

For example, we created a [Learning in Public Log](https://helengarden.netlify.app/feedback) for Civic Tech DC to document feedback we've received, our reflections, and any next steps taken. The goal is to make the decision-making process more transparent and show members that their perspective matters.
