Transitions are a normal part of running a civic tech project, and having a good handoff plan is important. It is essential to loop in the Civic Tech DC organizers to help with the process.

## Understand What You're Handing Off

Before making the plan, let's identify who is at the center of the handoff:

- **The project lead is stepping away**, and someone else will need to coordinate.
- **An important contributor is leaving:** someone who manages a big part of the project, like the database, website, research, or community outreach.
- **The project is ready for its community partner.** The handoff may involve training, documentation, and ongoing maintenance responsibilities.
- **The project is ending.** Even if development stops, the resources may still be useful to others.

## Start the Conversation Early

Whenever possible, begin planning a handoff before someone leaves or the project reaches its final milestone.

**If a project lead is leaving:** Talk with contributors and Civic Tech DC organizers about who might be interested in taking over. Don't assume the most active developer wants to become the next lead.

**If a contributor is leaving:** Ask what they're currently responsible for and ask them to document what they have been working on.

**If you're handing work to a community partner:** Ask who will use the tool and what support they need.

## Document the State of the Project

Before the handoff, prepare a short summary in a document.

| Information | What to document |
| --- | --- |
| Project purpose | What problem are you trying to solve, and for whom? |
| Current status | What's working, in development, or unfinished? |
| Key contacts | Who are the contributors, organizers, and community partners? |
| Upcoming priorities | What are the next two or three things that need attention? |
| Important decisions | What choices have already been made, and why? |
| Known challenges | What problems remain unresolved? |
| Resources | Where do the code, documentation, designs, and other materials live? |

Depending on the handoff, add a few more details:

- For a new project lead: Include meeting schedules, contributor availability, outstanding commitments, and how the team typically organizes work.
- For new contributors: Document what your responsibilities were and any login or account access they need.
- For a community partner: Include instructions for using the tool and updating content.
- For a closing project: Explain why the project stopped and what someone would need to know to restart or reuse the work.

## Communicate the Transition

Once the handoff is confirmed, let the project team, Civic Tech DC organizers, and any other appropriate folks know. The announcement doesn't need to be elaborately broadcast, but it should explain who's stepping away and when the transition will happen.

For contributors, this is also a good opportunity to recognize their contributions. Volunteers often spend significant time working behind the scenes, and that work deserves acknowledgment.

## When There Isn't Anyone to Take Over

Sometimes a project reaches a transition point and no one is ready or willing to take responsibility.

If this happens, reach out to Civic Tech DC organizers as soon as possible! They can help you figure out what needs immediate attention and explore next steps, whether that's finding a new lead, transferring the work to another team, or temporarily pausing the project.

## Project Handoff Checklist

Before considering a handoff complete, check that:

- The incoming lead, contributor, or partner has agreed to take over.
- Civic Tech DC organizers have been informed.
- Community partners know who their new contact is.
- The project's current status and next priorities are documented.
- Important files, repositories, and accounts are accessible.
- Ownership and administrative permissions have been updated.
- Outstanding commitments and deadlines have been discussed.
- Contributors know who to contact moving forward.
- The outgoing person's access has been reviewed.

Not every project will need every step. Use this checklist as a starting point and adapt it to your situation.
